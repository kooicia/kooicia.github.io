Waypoint: a travel companion app

Product spec · v0.1 draft · Oct 2026 · Mobile-first PWA
One line: TripIt tells you what you booked. Waypoint helps you get through the day: what's next, where it is, how to get there, and what you still need to do.

1. Problem

Trip info is spread across confirmation emails, a planning spreadsheet, Google Maps saved places, and notes. TripIt does a good job of collecting bookings, but it doesn't help much while you're on the trip:

  • It has no place for the unbooked parts of a day ("morning: Arashiyama, maybe lunch near the river").
  • It has no to-dos tied to trip days, like laundry, a visa appointment, shipping luggage ahead, or buying a transit card.
  • Getting from a booking to Google Maps directions takes too many taps, and addresses are hard to copy or show to a taxi driver.
  • It needs connectivity at the moments you have the least (airport, subway, roaming off).

2. Target user and context

A solo or small-group traveler who plans in a spreadsheet and wants a phone-friendly view of it while traveling. Most use is one-handed, on the go, often offline, and usually answers one of three questions:

  1. What's next, and when do I need to leave?
  2. Where is it? (tap → Google Maps, or show the address)
  3. What's my confirmation # / what do I still need to do?

3. Design principles

  • Today first. The app opens to the current trip day. Planning views come second.
  • One tap to Maps. Any item with a place has Map and Directions buttons that open the Google Maps app.
  • Copy everything. Confirmation numbers, addresses, and phone numbers can be copied with a tap.
  • Offline by default. All data lives on the device, and the app shell is cached. No account is needed.
  • Rough is fine. Every day has a free-text plan. Not everything has to be a timed item.

7. Platform

  • PWA: manifest + service worker (cache-first app shell). Installs from Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen, or Chrome → Install app. No App Store needed.
  • Storage: localStorage, kept on the device. On iOS, home-screen apps keep their own storage, separate from Safari's.
  • Hosting: static files. GitHub Pages works. HTTPS is required for the service worker.
  • No framework or build step in v0.1, so it's easy to change and host.

8. Out of scope (v0.1)

  • Accounts, cloud sync, shared trips with travel companions
  • Expense tracking and splitting
  • Booking or search

9. Open questions to pressure-test

  1. Is the spreadsheet the source of truth? If you keep planning in Sheets, a one-way "refresh from sheet" (P1) is better than paste-import and then editing in two places.
  2. Sync across devices. Is phone-only OK, or do you want to plan on a laptop and view on the phone? Phone-only keeps it backend-free. Laptop + phone needs a sheet sync or a small backend.
  3. Sharing. Do travel companions need a read-only view? A static share link (a JSON snapshot) is cheap to build. Collaborative editing is not.
  4. Time zones. v0.1 treats every time as local to where it happens, which matches how itineraries are written. Is that enough?
  5. Sensitive info. Passport and insurance numbers on the device: add a PIN lock, or just leave them out?
